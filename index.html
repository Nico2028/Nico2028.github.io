What's in my Name?

My last name comes first, as is tradition in Chinese culture. But it's my given name that feels almost... too on-brand.

The second character, Shū, usually means "comfort" or "relief." But if you break it down visually, it's made of two smaller parts: one means "to let go," the other means "to give."

The final character? It means "the people." So my full name kind of translates to: let go, give, for the people.

Identity & Mental Health

How my experiences in two vastly different worlds shaped how I see myself — and stay with myself

I’ve lived between two completely different worlds — the snowy stillness of Madison and the heat and pulse of Chongqing. My hometown only snowed once in 19 years. In Madison, I spent four winters walking across the frozen Mendota Lake.

After graduating college, I spent a year back in China. That’s when I started asking real questions — like where I want to build a life, what’s worth letting go of, and what actually matters. It was the first time I felt like I wasn’t just reacting, but really choosing.

My Core Motivation

Turning the mess into momentum, and loss into something that flows.

I didn’t grow up thinking I’d become a social worker. I never imagined myself as someone who could openly speak their truth. But over the past two years, life simply wouldn’t let me stay silent.

For as long as I can remember, my home carried tension. My mother endured domestic violence for decades, long before I knew the words for it. One night, when I was 14, she ran into my room, my father close behind. They circled my bed, yelling and grabbing, while I sat frozen. She climbed onto the mattress, called out my name, and begged me to save her. I was too young to protect anyone, yet too aware to remain untouched.

In May 2023, I graduated from college. I didn’t want to return home, but I had no choice. The company offering me an interview quickly backed away after discovering I’d need sponsorship to work legally in the U.S.

That September, my grandmother passed away. I held her hand until it turned cold. Days later, my rabbit Phil—my first pet, the emotional glue in a four-year relationship—died suddenly. Soon after, the relationship itself ended. Through all of this, I couldn’t tell my family anything. To them, I was their good, straight daughter stuck in the U.S. because of COVID—still the perfect student they always expected me to be. They didn't know the truth: that I’d long realized I liked girls, that my home in Madison felt safer than the one I had with them, and that I stayed away because I feared what they might think.

The losses piled up quickly, and the ground beneath my feet felt increasingly unstable. Meanwhile, my parents began discussing divorce, yet they still expected me to mediate—to maintain the illusion of peace. Quietly, I was falling apart, trapped in a house where I’d never felt safe, weighed down by grief nobody knew how to handle.

Finally, in October, I left. I flew to Busan to stay with my best friend, spending hours by the ocean, reflecting. I had no clear plans, only pressing questions: What do I truly want to do? What’s the core value that remains unshaken, no matter how much I lose?

Then I knew—I would pursue social work. Because I’ve lived in spaces where nobody knew how to ask for help, and I want to be the one who hears the silence and responds.

2001 · Home, Chongqing
My mom placed me—barely six months old—on my dad’s shoulder. I don’t remember this moment, but I’ve always carried the weight of being someone’s hope.
2005 · On a plane
I barely remember this trip. I just remember rushing toward the ocean—wild, fearless—and my dad catching me just in time.
2019 · Rural Indonesia
We restored a forgotten temple in a remote village. The quiet, the rhythm of work—it gave me a sense of shared dignity.
2019 · Yayasan Taman Permata Hati, Bali
There were so many kids, and just one woman they all called “Mom.” When they sang for us, it broke something open in me. It made me start wondering what care could look like.
2020 · Madison, WI
I liked drawing abstractly—because some emotions couldn’t take shape, only color and motion.
2021 · Groves behind Memorial Center at UW-Madison
I was struggling in college—torn between business and liberal arts. Every week I passed the Social Work School to get to my stats class, never imagining I’d one day belong there.
2021 · Madison, WI
This is Phil. He was my first pet and the heart of the home I built in my early 20s. In a time of lockdowns and loneliness, he taught me what it meant to care—and be cared for.
2023 · Busan, South Korea
This was when I seriously considered social work. That year I lost my grandma, my pet, and the “home” I built over four years. I realized what keeps me going is helping others through what I couldn’t go through alone.

I don’t know where this road ends, but I’m learning to walk it—with softness in my step, truth in my voice, and eyes open for those walking beside me.
